The Goal of This Lesson

This is not a lesson where students are expected to leave with the Trinity fully figured out. No one does. The goal is to give students a framework — the Trinity is real, it matters, and it tells us something specific about who God is and how he operates in the world.

The Scutum Fidei and the vocabulary are tools. The deeper work is helping students feel the weight of the story: a God who created everything, then stepped into his creation, then left his Spirit behind. That's the thread running through everything in this lesson.

Your Core Takeaway
"You don't need to fully grasp the Trinity, just like you don't need to fully grasp how the cells in our body work or how wifi somehow makes its way into our phones. But it is real, and it is important to know that our God has a way of working both in the material world and outside of it. He can create and do with his creation what he wants. That's why miracles are possible."

Teaching the Lesson — Step by Step
0–2 min Opening Prayer Slide 2
Do This Lead the class in the Sign of the Cross. Put Slide 2 on the screen. Read it aloud slowly, then hold 10–15 seconds of silence before speaking.

Don't rush the silence. Students this age rarely sit in quiet. Let it land before you move on. It sets the tone that this is a different kind of class.

2–10 min Watch Together Slide 3
Do This Play the Bible Project video directly from Slide 3 (~8 min). No setup needed. The video is embedded. Make sure the room has internet and sound before class.

After the video ends, before moving to the next slide, ask two or three students: "What was one thing you noticed? Did anything surprise you?" Accept all answers without correcting. This is a warm-up, not a test.

10–25 min The Big Idea + Three Persons Slides 4–6
Do This Slide 4 opens the discussion ("What do you already know?"). Give students 2 minutes to turn and talk, then call on a few. Slides 5–6 are teacher-led. Read through the cards on Slide 6 and add your own explanation for each Person.

Slide 6 is your biggest teaching moment before the diagram. When you get to God the Son, this is where you can bring the room forward. Students this age have likely studied Greek mythology and ancient history. Use that.

Your Takeaway — The Son
"What God that they've ever learned about came down from his throne to be spat on and crucified? Only ours. And what did he do after that? He left us the Holy Spirit to be with us in times of need. There is no better religion to be a part of than one where our God would lay down his life for us — who are so unworthy."
Signs It's Landing
Students asking follow-up questions. Comparing to what they've heard before. Nodding, leaning forward.
Signs They're Lost
Blank faces, side conversations. Slow down and ask: "Which part would you want me to say again differently?"

25–30 min The Shield of Faith Slide 7
Do This Put Slide 7 on the screen. Walk through the diagram first. Explain the solid lines (IS) and dashed lines (IS NOT). Then have students open their workbooks to Activity 2 and label the blank diagram using the word bank.

The IS / IS NOT language isn't just theological precision. It points to something students can actually observe in Scripture. When you explain it, anchor it to a scene they know.

Your Takeaway — IS / IS NOT
"The three Persons of the Trinity are of the same substance, but they are separate. That's why Jesus prays to God in the garden. His spiritual presence is not within this earthly world, so he prays. And that shows us something remarkable — prayer truly does cross through the physical realm into the spiritual realm. When Jesus prays, he's doing exactly what we do."
If a Student Says
"Wait — if Jesus IS God, why is he praying to God? Isn't that weird?"
That's exactly the right question. Jesus the Son is fully God. But he's a distinct Person from the Father. They are the same substance, but they are not the same Person. The Son, while on earth in a human body, prays to the Father. That's not a contradiction. That's the Trinity in action. And it means prayer is real. It goes somewhere.
30–43 min Scripture + Discussion Slides 8–10
Do This Read Matthew 28:19 aloud together from Slide 8. Then move through Slides 9–10. Let the discussion breathe. Don't rush to fill silence. Call on students who haven't spoken yet.

Discussion Question 2 is where the whole lesson converges. You're not just asking a theology question. You're asking students to feel the weight of the story. Let them sit with it.

Your Takeaway — The Discussion Goal
"What you want them to land on — even if they can't say it yet — is this: 'God created me, loved me, and then died for me. Why wouldn't I follow him?' That's the question you're trying to plant. You don't need them to answer it out loud. You just need it to be the thing they're sitting with when they leave."
Signs It's Landing
Personal answers. Students referencing their own experience of prayer, or people they know. Quiet after a response is thinking, not boredom.
Signs They're Lost
One-word answers ("I don't know"). Try asking: "Has anyone ever felt like God was close? Or far away? Tell us about that."
43–55 min Workbook Activities Slide 11
Do This Put Slide 11 on the screen and give students 10–12 minutes to work through Activities 1–3 in their workbooks. Walk the room. Sit with students who are stuck on the prayer. Early finishers can write a second prayer to a different Person.

Activity 3, the prayer, is not a writing assignment. Don't grade it. If a student says they don't know what to write, ask them: "What's one thing you want to say to God today? Just write that."

When Students Don't Get It

Some student is going to push back — "this doesn't make sense" or "how can God be three things at once?" That's not a problem. That's a good student. Here's how to meet it.

Your Answer for the Skeptic
"It shouldn't make sense to you. If it was easy enough for all of us to understand, everyone in the world would be Christian. You don't need the answers to everything in the universe — but you should trust that a God who could create the universe can also work within it. He showed that with every miracle Jesus performed. We aren't expected to know how it happens. Just that he did it for us. And that's not a small thing."
If a Student Says
"My parents say all religions are basically the same."
You can say: "Most religions have a god who is powerful and far away. Ours is different. Our God stepped into history as a person, lived a real life, was executed, and came back. You can disagree with that — but it's not the same story as every other religion. It's a very specific claim."
If a Student Says
"Why does it matter if God is one Person or three?"
Because it changes who God is. If God is just one Person alone, he needed to create us to have something to love. But if God is Father, Son, and Holy Spirit — three Persons in a relationship of love — then he didn't need us. He chose us. That's a different kind of love entirely.
If a Student Says
"I prayed once and nothing happened."
Don't dismiss it. Say: "I've felt that too. Most people have. Prayer isn't a vending machine — but it is real. Jesus prayed in the garden the night before he died, and he didn't get the answer he asked for either. That didn't make the prayer meaningless. Keep going."
