The Goal of This Lesson

Nobody leaves this lesson with the Trinity figured out, and they aren't supposed to. The goal is a framework: the Trinity is real, it matters, and it tells us who God is. Three Persons, one God, loving each other before anything else existed.

The second half turns that belief into a command. If God is a family of love, Jesus's New Commandment is the invitation to join it: love one another the way he loves us. Keep the two halves connected. The Trinity is what God is. The New Commandment is what that means for how we live.

Your Core Takeaway
"You don't need to fully grasp the Trinity, just like you don't need to fully grasp how the cells in our body work or how wifi somehow makes its way into our phones. But it is real, and it is important to know that our God has a way of working both in the material world and outside of it. He can create and do with his creation what he wants. That's why miracles are possible."
About the Image Slides

Eight slides are just a title and a picture. They're there so students look up instead of reading. On those slides, the words come from you. Each one has a Say This box below with a short voiceover. Say it in your own words, then point at the picture as you go. The same script is in the slide's Presenter Notes (press N while presenting).

Each Say This box starts with a one-line description of the picture. If an image ever fails to load, read that line aloud and keep going.

Teaching the Lesson, Step by Step
0–2 min Opening Prayer Slide 1
Do This Lead the Sign of the Cross slowly, as a class. Tell them there are two big ideas today: who God is (three Persons, one God), and what Jesus asks us to do about it (love each other the way he loves us).

2–5 min Before We Begin Slide 2
Do This Read the question and let it sit in silence: If God is love, who did he love before he made anything? Take a few answers without correcting any of them. Tell them the class will answer it by Slide 12.
5–8 min Trinity Slide 3
Do This The headline of the whole lesson. Say it slowly: three Persons, one God. Each one fully God. None greater, none first. Walk through the three and add your own explanation for each Person. When you get to the Son, this is where you can bring the room forward.
Your Takeaway: The Son
"What God that they've ever learned about came down from his throne to be spat on and crucified? Only ours. And what did he do after that? He left us the Holy Spirit to be with us in times of need. There is no better religion to be a part of than one where our God would lay down his life for us — who are so unworthy."
8–11 min Three Persons, One God Slide 4 · Image
Say This
On screen: Andrei Rublev's icon of the Trinity. Three figures around one table.
A monk named Andrei Rublev painted this icon in Russia around 1411. It shows the three visitors who came to Abraham's tent in Genesis 18, and Christians have seen the Trinity in them ever since. Look at the faces. They're the same. Three Persons, one God.
Ask: What do you notice about how they're sitting? (They lean toward each other. They share one table. Hold onto that. It comes back on Slide 15.)

11–13 min IS and IS NOT Slide 5
Do This Keep it to the two lines on the slide. Each Person IS God. No Person IS another Person. They aren't three gods, and they aren't one Person wearing three masks.
Your Takeaway: IS / IS NOT
"The three Persons of the Trinity are of the same substance, but they are separate. That's why Jesus prays to God in the garden. His spiritual presence is not within this earthly world, so he prays. And that shows us something remarkable — prayer truly does cross through the physical realm into the spiritual realm. When Jesus prays, he's doing exactly what we do."
If a Student Says
"Wait, if Jesus IS God, why is he praying to God? Isn't that weird?"
That's exactly the right question. Jesus the Son is fully God. But he's a distinct Person from the Father. They are the same substance, but they are not the same Person. The Son, while on earth in a human body, prays to the Father. That's not a contradiction. That's the Trinity in action. And it means prayer is real. It goes somewhere.
13–16 min The Shield of the Trinity Slide 6 · Image
Say This
On screen: the Shield of the Trinity diagram. God in the center, the three Persons at the corners.
Christians have drawn this diagram for about 800 years. Let's trace it. From the center, every Person connects to God with the word IS. The Father is God. The Son is God. The Holy Spirit is God. Around the outside, the Persons connect to each other with IS NOT. The Father is not the Son. The Son is not the Holy Spirit.
Ask: Can someone come up and trace one IS line and one IS NOT line for us?

If your workbook has the Shield of Faith activity, this is the moment to point to it. It's the same diagram.

16–18 min Matthew 28:19 Slide 7
Do This Read the verse aloud together. Ask: how many names? One. Jesus doesn't say "in the names of." One name, belonging to three Persons.
18–19 min In the Name Slide 8 · Image
Say This
On screen: a priest pouring water over a baby at the font.
Most of you were baptized as babies. A priest or deacon poured water over your head and said the exact words Jesus gave in Matthew 28: in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. You've belonged to the Trinity since that day.
19–21 min The Sign of the Cross Slide 9
Do This Have everyone make the Sign of the Cross slowly, together. Match each phrase to the motion. "In the name" is one God. "Father, Son, and Holy Spirit" is three Persons. Christians have prayed this way for close to 2,000 years.
21–23 min St. Patrick's Shamrock Slide 10 · Image
Say This
On screen: St. Patrick on an Irish hillside, holding up a shamrock to a group of villagers.
The story goes that St. Patrick picked a shamrock off the ground to explain the Trinity to the people of Ireland. Three leaves, one plant. It's a good start, but every picture of the Trinity breaks somewhere. A leaf is only part of the shamrock, and the Son isn't part of God. He is fully God.
Ask: Can anyone spot where the shamrock falls short?

23–26 min The Frozen Lake Slide 11 · Image
Say This
On screen: one frozen lake, cut away so you can see all three layers. Mist rising above, ice on top, water and fish underneath.
The shamrock is a good start, but it falls short. Here's a picture I like a little better: a frozen lake on a cold morning. A fish swimming underneath knows the lake as water. A rabbit hopping across the top only feels the ice under its feet. It never sees the water below or the mist above its head. A bird flying overhead sees the mist rising off the lake, but has no idea there's ice and water underneath. It's all one lake. The lake is God. The water is the Father, the ice is the Son, and the mist is the Holy Spirit.
Optional: Even this picture breaks. The Father, Son, and Holy Spirit aren't three ways of seeing God, depending on where you stand. Each one is fully God, all the time, and they really are three different Persons.

Point to each layer as you name it. The picture works because all three are there at the same time, in one lake. If a student asks whether the ice melts into water, that's the spot where this picture breaks, and it's worth saying so.

26–28 min 1 John 4:8 Slide 12
Do This Go back to the opening question. Who did God love before he made anything? Himself, as Trinity. The Father loves the Son, the Son loves the Father, and the Holy Spirit is that love. God didn't start loving when he made us.
28–29 min A Family of Love Slide 13 · Image
Say This
On screen: a triquetra, three loops of gold light woven together.
Three loops woven together, with no beginning and no end. This knot is called a triquetra. Before there was a world, there was already love: the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit, loving each other forever. That's the answer to our opening question.

29–34 min Discussion Slide 14
Do This Start with "Which Person of the Trinity do you think about most?" Then give a few students a try at the starred question: how would you explain the Trinity to a friend? Let them use the shield, the shamrock, or the lake.
Signs It's Landing
Students using IS and IS NOT on their own. Picking a picture and saying where it breaks.
Signs They're Lost
Blank faces, side conversations. Slow down and ask: "Which part would you want me to say again differently?"
34–42 min Holy Trinity Trifold Keep Slide 14 up
Do This Hand out the trifold folded, GOD side up. Point out the cover: one God. Have them lift the cover and do the IS or IS NOT fill-in first, then unfold it to meet the three Persons and check their answers against the IS NOT labels on the folds. Give the rest of the time to the three prompts inside. Early finishers can practice the Sign of the Cross steps on the back.

The fold does the teaching. Folded up, there's one GOD. Opened, there are three Persons, each labeled IS GOD, with IS NOT on the folds between them. Say that out loud once while they're unfolding it.

42–43 min A Seat at the Table Slide 15 · Image
Say This
On screen: a candlelit table set for a meal, with the near side open, facing us.
Think back to Rublev's icon. The table has an open side, facing us. Many people think he left it open on purpose. God's love isn't a closed circle. There's a seat for you. Jesus doesn't just want us to believe in that love. He wants us to join it, and on his last night he told his friends how.

43–44 min The Last Supper Slide 16 · Image
Say This
On screen: Jesus kneeling to wash Peter's feet.
It's Jesus's last night before the Cross. He gets down on his knees and washes his friends' dirty feet. That was a servant's job. Peter can't believe it. Then Jesus stands up and gives them a new commandment.
44–47 min John 13:34–35 Slide 17
Do This The heart of the second half. Read it slowly, twice if needed. He said this at the Last Supper, hours before his arrest.
47–49 min What Makes It NEW? Slide 18
Do This "Love your neighbor" was already in Leviticus 19:18. The command to love isn't new. The standard is: "as I have loved you." Jesus's own love, all the way to the Cross, is the measuring stick now.
49–51 min What Does This Look Like? Slide 19
Do This Make it concrete. After the three examples on the slide, ask for one more from their own week. Don't rush this slide.

51–52 min Love One Another Slide 20 · Image
Say This
On screen: a lunchroom. Two kids at the end of an empty table, one who was alone and one who chose to sit there.
What do you see? One kid eating alone. One kid who chose to sit down next to them. Nobody made them do it.
Ask: What did it cost? What did it give the kid who was alone? That's what "as I have loved you" looks like at lunch.
52–57 min Discussion Slide 21
Do This Ask how the two halves connect: God is a family of love, and Jesus asks us to love the same way. The starred question is the take-home in disguise. Let it lead into the last slide.
Your Takeaway: The Discussion Goal
"What you want them to land on — even if they can't say it yet — is this: 'God created me, loved me, and then died for me. Why wouldn't I follow him?' That's the question you're trying to plant. You don't need them to answer it out loud. You just need it to be the thing they're sitting with when they leave."
57–60 min Take-Home + Closing Prayer Slide 22
Do This Read the take-home: love one person this week the way Jesus loves you, even when it's not easy, and come back ready to share what happened. Close with the Glory Be. It's a prayer to all three Persons, which makes it the right way to end this lesson.

When Students Don't Get It

Some student is going to push back: "this doesn't make sense," or "how can God be three things at once?" That's not a problem. That's a good student. Here's how to meet it.

Your Answer for the Skeptic
"It shouldn't make sense to you. If it was easy enough for all of us to understand, everyone in the world would be Christian. You don't need the answers to everything in the universe — but you should trust that a God who could create the universe can also work within it. He showed that with every miracle Jesus performed. We aren't expected to know how it happens. Just that he did it for us. And that's not a small thing."
If a Student Says
"My parents say all religions are basically the same."
You can say: "Most religions have a god who is powerful and far away. Ours is different. Our God stepped into history as a person, lived a real life, was executed, and came back. You can disagree with that, but it's not the same story as every other religion. It's a very specific claim."
If a Student Says
"Why does it matter if God is one Person or three?"
Because it changes who God is. If God is just one Person alone, he needed to create us to have something to love. But if God is Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, three Persons in a relationship of love, then he didn't need us. He chose us. That's a different kind of love entirely. (This is the opening question from Slide 2, from the other side.)
If a Student Says
"So the Trinity is like water, ice, and steam?"
Close, and it's a good picture, but it breaks in one place. Water turns into ice and then into steam. God doesn't switch between being the Father, the Son, and the Spirit. All three are fully God, all the time, at once. That's why the frozen lake has all three there together.
If a Student Says
"I prayed once and nothing happened."
Don't dismiss it. Say: "I've felt that too. Most people have. Prayer isn't a vending machine, but it is real. Jesus prayed in the garden the night before he died, and he didn't get the answer he asked for either. That didn't make the prayer meaningless. Keep going."
