The Goal of This Lesson

Last week ended on a cliffhanger: God gave Adam and Eve real freedom, and they used it to choose wrong. This week answers what happened next, and it answers it fast. God's very first move after the first sin wasn't punishment. It was a promise. That order matters more than any other single fact in the lesson.

The second half shifts from what went wrong to what helps us choose better right now: the Holy Spirit, our conscience, and the Fruits that show the Spirit is actually working in us. Twelve fruits is a lot to hold onto. Don't aim for students to memorize the list today. Aim for them to recognize a few by name when they see one in their own life this week.

Your Core Takeaway
"A relationship with God doesn't build itself. Just like any relationship, it takes showing up for it, every day. If you want to be a great athlete, you have to put in the work: study the playbook, listen to your coaches, and practice. Our relationship with God works the same way. The more we learn about who he is, spend time with him in prayer, and read his word, the easier it gets to see which path we should take. And the clearer his voice gets when our conscience is trying to guide us there."

Teaching the Lesson: Step by Step
0–2 min Opening Prayer Slide 1
Do This Lead the Sign of the Cross with the title slide on screen. Remind students in one sentence: last week covered creation and God as Father. Today picks up right where that left off.
2–4 min Before We Begin Slide 2
Do This Read the question aloud: "Adam and Eve had everything in the garden. So why wasn't it enough?" Take a few answers without correcting them. Don't resolve it. It's the question the whole lesson answers.
4–7 min Free Will, Revisited Slide 3
Do This Recap Week 1 in one line: God gave real free will so love could be chosen, not forced. Then bridge to Pinocchio, setting up the video on the next slide. Don't explain the comparison yet. Let the video make it first.
Your Takeaway: The Pinocchio Bridge
"God gave us free will so we could be more than just puppets on strings. Do you know the story of Pinocchio? He was a wooden puppet who wanted to become a real boy. Part of becoming real was being given a conscience, a little voice named Jiminy Cricket, to help him tell right from wrong. God gives every one of us that same gift for real. He gave us free will so we could truly choose to love him. But a choice only means something if you could have chosen differently. That's also why we're able to choose wrong."

7–13 min The Video + Two Voices, One Choice Slides 4–5
Prep Before Class Slide 4's video field is blank by default. Pick a short clip of Honest John the Fox and "Give a Little Whistle," and paste the URL in before you teach this.
Do This Play the clip. Keep it short. Move straight to Slide 5's side-by-side comparison, Fox and Serpent, Cricket and Holy Spirit, and let the parallel do the work.

Then ask: "Pinocchio didn't always listen to Jiminy Cricket. Do you always listen to your conscience?" A few honest "no" answers are the point, not a problem to fix.

13–18 min What Temptation Feels Like Slide 6
Do This Name the distinction first: temptation is the pull toward something wrong, not the wrong thing itself. Then tell your own story below in your own words. Ask afterward: "Has that ever happened to you?"
Your Takeaway: The Video Game Story
"Temptation isn't the same thing as sin. It's the pull toward it, and everybody feels that pull. When I was a kid, a video game I was dying to play came out, so I faked being sick to stay home from school. I knew my parents would have to leave for work, which meant I'd have the whole day to play. The temptation for me was a video game. I knew lying to my parents was wrong, but I wanted to play that badly. I felt so guilty afterward that I only played for less than an hour before I turned off the console and went back to bed. That's what temptation feels like: wanting something so much you talk yourself into believing the wrong choice is worth it."
If a Student Says
"Isn't it just as bad to be tempted as it is to actually do it?"
No, and that's worth saying clearly. Feeling the pull isn't a sin. Scripture actually says Jesus himself was tempted in the desert for forty days and never sinned (Matthew 4:1–11). Being tempted isn't a failure. What you do with it is the part that counts.

18–26 min The Story, In Order + Genesis 3:15 Slides 7–8
Do This Walk the timeline on Slide 7 row by row. Land on the last row on purpose: the promise of a Savior comes in the very same scene as the sin, not later. Move to Slide 8 and read Genesis 3:15 aloud yourself, slowly.

A strike to the head is fatal. A strike to the heel is a real wound, but not final. That's the shape of the promise: victory, at a real cost, given the instant it's needed.

Your Takeaway: What the Sin Actually Broke
"In the Garden of Eden, God told Adam and Eve not to eat from one specific tree, but he didn't spell out every detail of what would happen if they did. He could have described the exact punishment in advance, so there'd be no chance they'd risk it. He didn't, on purpose. Having a real choice is what makes obedience mean something. When Adam and Eve ate the fruit anyway, what broke wasn't just a rule. It was trust."
26–30 min Salvation Slide 9
Do This Define salvation plainly: God forgiving our sins and restoring our friendship with him, through Jesus. Say clearly that God never stopped loving Adam and Eve, and never stops loving a student for making a mistake either.
Your Takeaway: God Didn't Walk Away
"God never stopped loving Adam and Eve just because they disobeyed him, and he will never stop loving you when you make mistakes either. That doesn't mean he just hands out a free pass to keep sinning forever. Forgiveness cost him everything. He gave his one and only Son, Jesus, to make that forgiveness possible. And God didn't wait to promise this. The very same moment Adam and Eve sinned, he had already promised a Savior was coming."
If a Student Says
"If God knew they'd sin, why didn't he just stop them?"
Bring it back to Week 1: love that's forced isn't real love. God wanted Adam and Eve to choose him freely, the same way he wants that from you. A love that couldn't have gone wrong wouldn't have been a real choice at all.

30–37 min The Fruits of the Holy Spirit + Galatians Slides 10–11
Do This Bridge with the question on Slide 10: how do we choose better than Adam and Eve did? Tell the apple farm story below, then move to Slide 11 and read Galatians 5:22–23 aloud. Point out that Paul writes "fruit," singular, not "fruits."
Your Takeaway: The Apple Farm
"I worked on a friend's apple farm every summer when I was growing up, and here's something interesting about apple trees: if you just let one grow wild, it'll still produce fruit. Just small, bitter crab apples. To grow good, sweet apples, you have to prune it. We had to cut off the branches that shot straight up, because they were stealing the nutrients the good branches needed. God asks us to do the same thing with our own lives, pruning away the things that pull us toward temptation and away from him. But he doesn't leave us to do that pruning alone. When Jesus rose from the dead, he left us a helper: the Holy Spirit."
If a Student Says
"How do I know it's really my conscience talking, and not just what I want to do?"
Good question, and worth taking seriously. God is always calling us toward good, because he is good, all the time. The more we study the life of Jesus, the more we recognize his voice, the same way you can grow so close to someone you can finish each other's sentences. That's a relationship you build over time, not something you're handed all at once.
37–49 min The Twelve Fruits, In Detail Slides 12, 16–18
Do This Slide 12 shows all twelve at once, as a full harvest. Then work through Slides 16–18 in three rounds of four, reading each fruit's kid-level example aloud. After each round, ask for a show of hands: who's done this recently without realizing it was one of the Fruits?

Chastity is the one that needs the most care. Keep it to respect and purity of heart, nothing more: not saying unkind things about how someone looks, treating your own body and other people's with respect. Don't go further than that at this age.

If a Student Says
"Why is chastity on the list? Isn't that a grown-up word?"
Keep your answer short and age-appropriate: it means respecting yourself and others, and keeping your thoughts, words, and actions honest. That's the whole answer for this age. You don't need to say more, and you shouldn't.
Signs It's Landing
Hands going up for the "who's done this" check. Students connecting a fruit to a specific person they know, a teacher, a sibling, a friend.
Signs They're Lost
Twelve names blurring together. Slow down and pick just three to really land: kindness, patience, self-control. The rest can stay lighter.

49–53 min Examination of Conscience Slide 13
Do This Walk through the questions on the slide, then make it real with your own nightly routine below. This is what turns tonight's take-home from an assignment into a habit worth keeping.
Your Takeaway: A Nightly Habit
"Every night when I pray, I start by thanking God for the good things from my day, maybe it was something big, like a promotion, or something small, like a presentation going well or a kind word from someone. But then I do something called an Examination of Conscience: I look back and tell God, honestly, where I could have done better. I compare how I lived that day to how Jesus lived and how he told us to treat one another. When I fall short, I tell God I'm sorry and ask him to help me listen to my conscience better tomorrow."
53–57 min Discussion Slide 14
Do This Start with the Pinocchio question, it calls back to Slide 5. Work down the list. The starred question is the stretch challenge if the class still has energy.

Don't rush the silence after a question. Call on students who haven't spoken yet before you rephrase.

When Students Don't Get It

This lesson covers a lot of ground fast. A student who pushes back on the pacing or the logic is paying attention. Here's how to meet the ones most likely to come up.

If a Student Says
"If God forgave Adam and Eve right away, why do we still have to say sorry when we mess up?"
Because forgiveness is offered, but a relationship still needs both people showing up. God's promise was immediate. Our own turning back to him, admitting what we did, asking for help, is how we actually receive it. That's what the Examination of Conscience is for.
If a Student Says
"Why do bad things still happen if the Holy Spirit is helping us?"
The Spirit doesn't take away free will, ours or anyone else's. He gives us the help to choose well. People still get to choose badly, and sometimes they do. That's not the Spirit failing. It's the same freedom Week 1 was about, still in play.
If a Student Says
"Isn't self-control just about not eating too much candy?"
That's a real example, and a fine place to start. Push it one step further: self-control is saying no to anything you want in the moment because you know it isn't good for you, or isn't good for someone else. Candy is the easy version. Most of the time it shows up in smaller, harder moments, like walking away from a fight instead of saying something you'd regret.
