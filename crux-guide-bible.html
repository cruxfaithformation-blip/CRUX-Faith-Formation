The Goal of This Lesson

Four weeks in, the class has met God, the Fall, the Trinity, and Jesus. This week they get the book all of it comes from, and two skills to go with it: finding any verse on their own, and seeing the Bible as one story told across 73 books. If a student leaves able to open a Bible and find John 3:16 without help, the lesson worked.

The second half sets up the rest of the year. The timeline on Slides 14–15 shows the gap between the Fall and Jesus. Abraham, Moses, and David fill it, starting next week.

Your Core Takeaway
"Seventy-three books. About forty writers. One Author. From the first page to the last, the Bible is God making a promise and keeping it. And when you open it, he's still talking to you."
On the Final Inventory
Q5 The Exodus: “The story of Moses leading the Hebrews out of slavery in Egypt.” Point to Egypt and Sinai on the map and tell students they will hear the whole story next week.
Q13 The Lord’s Prayer: “Write the Lord’s Prayer.” Keep praying it together every class.
Teaching the Lesson: Step by Step
0–2 min Opening Prayer Slide 1
Do This Lead the Sign of the Cross. Then: "Today we're talking about a book you've definitely seen, probably own, and maybe have never really opened. By the end of class, you'll know how to find anything in it."
2–4 min The Best-Seller Slide 2
Do This Ask what the best-selling book of all time is. Take three or four guesses. Someone will say Harry Potter. Then reveal it: the Bible, billions of copies, more than any other book in history. End on the twist: "And it isn't even one book."

4–8 min A Library on Scrolls Slides 3–4
Do This On Slide 3, count the 73 spines together and point out that each color is a different kind of book. On Slide 4, hold up your papyrus scroll before you say anything. Explain papyrus (a paper made from a reed plant along the Nile) and parchment (animal skin), then show the Great Isaiah Scroll and the 24-foot strip of the whole book unrolled. Start the scroll moving around the room while you talk.

Get the prop wording right. The Great Isaiah Scroll on screen is parchment. Your scroll is papyrus. When you point at the picture, say "a scroll like this," not "papyrus like this."

Luke 4:17–20 is the hook for this age: Jesus himself "unrolled the scroll and found the passage" in his hometown synagogue. He read from a scroll just like the one they're holding.

8–13 min One Author, One Story Slides 5–6
Do This Slide 5: read "All scripture is inspired by God" (2 Timothy 3:16) and use the coach and quarterback picture. The coach calls the play, but the quarterback throws it in his own style. Ask: in the Caravaggio painting, who's really writing? Slide 6: point to the four numbered arcs one at a time and read each pair of verses from the notes.

Land the arc chart with the honest version. Some writers quoted older books on purpose, like quoting a favorite movie. But Abraham didn't know about Jesus, and the Roman soldiers weren't trying to fulfill a psalm. "Forty writers couldn't plan that. One Author could."

13–18 min The Shelves Slides 7–10
Do This Move quickly here. These four slides are a map, and the kids will use it in ten minutes. Slide 7: Old Testament, 46 books, everything before Jesus. New Testament, 27 books, Jesus and the Church. Plant one word: covenant, a promise between God and his people. Slides 8–9: name the shelves with one familiar example each. Slide 10: the four Gospels and their symbols (man, lion, ox, eagle).

Save "covenant." It's the whole story next week, when Abraham, Moses, and David each receive one.

18–23 min Find a Book, Find a Verse Slides 11–12
Do This Hold up a real Bible for this whole step. Show the table of contents first. Then show the big picture with your thumb: Old Testament is the big chunk in front, and the New Testament starts about three-quarters of the way through, at Matthew. Point to the book name printed at the top of each page. Find Exodus together (right after Genesis), then Galatians (past the Gospels and Acts, after Romans and the two Corinthians). Teach the trick for the next four letters: Go Eat Pop Corn, Galatians, Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians.

On Slide 12, say the order out loud and have them repeat it: book, big number, little number. Chapter is the big number, verse is the small one. Then race: "Who can find Exodus first? Now Galatians." The race is the practice round for the treasure hunt.

If a Student Says
"My Bible doesn't have the same page numbers as yours."
That's why we use chapter and verse instead of page numbers. Every Bible is printed differently, but John 3:16 is John 3:16 in all of them. That's exactly the problem the chapter and verse numbers were invented to solve.

23–38 min Treasure Hunt Slide 13
Do This Put students in pairs with one Bible and one Treasure Hunt handout. Read the rules on the slide together. Pairs go to the stops in order, copy one line from each verse onto the map, then follow the trail to the treasure. When a pair finds a stop, they read their line aloud and the class guesses which week it came from. When most pairs reach John 3:16, stop everyone and read it out loud together.

1Jeremiah 1:5Week 1, Who Is God?

3Matthew 28:19Week 3, The Holy Trinity & the New Commandment
4John 1:14Week 4, Who Is Jesus Christ?

Bonus1 John 4:8Week 3, for early finishers

Timing. About two minutes to pair up and explain, ten for the lookups, and a few to read the treasure together. The first stop is slow. By stop four most pairs are fast. If you're behind, give each pair only two stops (1–2 or 3–4), then find John 3:16 as a whole class.

Watch stop 2 and the bonus. Galatians is the first stop in the New Testament letters, so expect pairs to get lost in the Gospels. Send them back to the Slide 11 path: past Acts, Romans, 1 and 2 Corinthians. On the bonus, kids land in the Gospel of John chapter 4 instead of the letter 1 John near the very back.

The answer key with the full NABRE text of every verse is on page 2 of the handout.

Signs It's Landing
Pairs opening to the table of contents on their own. Kids saying "Old Testament" or "New Testament" before they flip. Partners correcting each other on chapter versus verse.
Signs They're Lost
Flipping from the front one page at a time. Mixing up chapter and verse (landing in John 16:3). Kneel down with the pair and walk one stop with them: book, big number, little number.

38–41 min God's Story Slides 14–15
Do This Slide 14 is the whole Bible in one line: God makes the world, we break it, God makes a promise, and he keeps it all the way to Jesus. Then the Church carries the story to us. On Slide 15, show where our weeks sit on that line. Weeks 1–2 were the beginning, Week 4 was Jesus, and the gap in the middle is next week.

Keep Slide 15 handy for the rest of the year. Bringing it back at the start of a class shows students where they are in the story.

41–44 min Where It Happened Slide 16
Do This Point and name the five places on the slide: Egypt, Mount Sinai, Jerusalem, Bethlehem, Nazareth. Then click Open the interactive map in the bottom-right corner. Use Next place to fly to each one. Every stop shows a verse and what the place is called today. If there's time, switch to From space at Mount Sinai, or Roman times at Bethlehem.

The goal here is "these are real places you could visit." Save the journeys themselves for next week. The map at cruxfaith.com/crux-map-bible.html also has Paul's missionary journeys for later in the year.

44–46 min We Hear It Every Sunday Slide 17
Do This Connect the Bible to Mass. Every Mass has a first reading, a Psalm, a second reading, and the Gospel, and everyone stands for the Gospel. Teach the two responses and practice each one twice, out loud, with you as reader and priest.

Reader: The Word of the Lord. All: Thanks be to God.
Priest: The Gospel of the Lord. All: Praise to you, Lord Jesus Christ.

46–52 min Look at the Birds Slide 18
Do This Before you reveal the slide, say: "Everybody, find Matthew 6:26. Go." The first student to find it reads it aloud. Then reveal the slide and explain: Jesus said this to a crowd of worried people. Birds don't plant anything or keep a fridge, and they wake up singing anyway, because God takes care of them.

"Look at the birds in the sky; they do not sow or reap, they gather nothing into barns, yet your heavenly Father feeds them. Are not you more important than they?" (Matthew 6:26)

Then connect it to them. The Bible calls God's word "living and effective" (Hebrews 4:12). A verse from 2,000 years ago can speak to a test, tryouts, or something going on at home this week. Ask: what's something people your age worry about?

Your Story: A Time This Verse Found You Before class, write down a time Matthew 6:26 (or any verse) spoke to something you were worried about. Keep it short and specific. Tell it right after the verse is read, before you ask the class about their worries.
52–56 min Favorite Bible Story Slide 19
Do This The slide lists twelve well-known stories to jog memories, so more hands go up. Students can pick one of those or any story they love. After each answer, ask: "Which shelf is it on?" The shelf for every story on the list is in the presenter notes. A Gospel story is a good chance to name which of the four Gospels it's from.
56–60 min Take-Home + Closing Prayer Slide 20
Do This Assign Genesis 12:1–3 for tonight: "Find it using what you learned today, and come back knowing who Abraham is." Point to the stars on the slide as a hint. God promised Abraham a family as many as the stars. Close with the Glory Be.

When Students Don't Get It
If a Student Says
"If people wrote it, how is it God's word?"
Go back to the coach and the quarterback. The Church teaches that the Bible has two kinds of authors: God, and the real human writers, who wrote in their own words and style (Catechism 105–106). Matthew sounds like Matthew, and Paul sounds like Paul, but God is the one calling the play.
If a Student Says
"My friend's Bible only has 66 books. Why does ours have 73?"
Most Protestant Bibles leave out seven Old Testament books that Catholics have always read: Tobit, Judith, Wisdom, Sirach, Baruch, and 1 and 2 Maccabees. Your friend's Bible is still the Bible. Ours just has the whole library. Keep it kind and simple; this isn't the day for the full history.
If a Student Says
"Did all of it really happen? Like Jonah and the big fish?"
Remember the shelves. Some books are history, some are songs and poems, some are prophets, some are letters. The Church asks us to read each one as the kind of book it is (Catechism 110). A song and a history book tell the truth in different ways, and every book teaches us something true about God. If a student wants more on a specific story, say you'll talk about it after class.
If a Student Says
"Who decided which books go in the Bible?"
The Church, guided by the Holy Spirit, over the first few hundred years after Jesus. Christians read many writings, and the Church recognized which ones were inspired by God. That's the list we still use.
