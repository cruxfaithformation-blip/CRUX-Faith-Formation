Feast Day · May 30

Saint Joan of Arc

Patron of France and of soldiers.

Hard to Believe, But True

A Farm Girl Led an Army.
She Was Seventeen.

No armor training. No royal family. No army of her own. Just a teenage girl from a tiny village who believed God was asking her to do something enormous, and who said yes. By the time she was nineteen, kings and soldiers all knew her name.

Domrémy, France · Around 1412

An Ordinary Girl in a Country at War

Jeanne d'Arc grew up in a small farming village called Domrémy. She helped with chores, tended sheep, and went to Mass like everyone else. But France was in the middle of a long, brutal war with England, called the Hundred Years' War, and it had already dragged on for decades. Whole villages like hers lived in fear of soldiers passing through.

Around Age Thirteen

Something Started Calling Her

Joan later testified that she began hearing voices she believed belonged to Saint Michael the Archangel, Saint Catherine of Alexandria, and Saint Margaret of Antioch. At first they told her to be good and to go to church often. Over time, she said, they told her something much bigger: that she had to help save France, and help Charles, the uncrowned heir to the throne, become king.

The Church doesn't ask us to be certain about the details of anyone's private visions, including Joan's. What the Church later recognized, when it named her a saint, was something we can be certain about: her faith was real, and she followed where she believed God was leading her, even when it cost her everything.

1429

Convincing a Prince to Trust a Peasant Girl

Joan left home and traveled to find Charles, the Dauphin (the French word for the heir to the throne). It was not an easy journey. She had to convince guards, then noblemen, then finally Charles himself, that God had truly sent her. Charles had her questioned by churchmen for weeks before he agreed. Eventually, he gave her something remarkable for a seventeen-year-old farm girl: soldiers, armor, and a place at the head of his army.

Joan didn't fight like a typical soldier. She carried a banner, not a sword, and led from the front, but her presence changed how the French army believed in itself. What had felt hopeless for years suddenly felt possible.

1430–1431

Captured, and Judged Unfairly

About a year after Orléans, Joan was captured during a battle near the town of Compiègne. She was eventually handed over to the English, who wanted her out of the way for good. They put her on trial in the city of Rouen, in front of a court of churchmen who were loyal to England, not to the truth. The trial was not fair. Joan, still a teenager, stood alone and answered hard questions with remarkable steadiness and faith.

1431, and Then 1456

Killed Unjustly, Later Cleared

In May 1431, when Joan was only about nineteen, that unfair court had her put to death. It is one of the saddest parts of her story, and it happened because she refused to say her faith and her calling weren't real.

But her story doesn't end there. Twenty-five years later, Pope Callixtus III ordered a new trial to look at everything again. In 1456, that court threw out the first verdict completely and declared Joan innocent. The Church itself said, clearly and publicly, that what had been done to her was wrong. Justice came late, but it came.

Canonized 1920

Why the Church Calls Her a Saint

It's tempting to think Joan is a saint because she won battles. She isn't. Plenty of people in history have led armies. Joan is a saint because of what was underneath all of it: total trust in God, courage to follow him even when powerful people doubted her, and a faith that never broke, not even when she was scared, alone, and on trial for her life.

  • ✓ She listened for God's call, even as a young teenager
  • ✓ She obeyed, even when it was frightening and costly
  • ✓ She stayed faithful to the very end, and was proven right

Her Own Words, at Her Trial

What She Said Under Pressure

"If I am not in a state of grace, may God put me there; and if I am, may God so keep me. I should be the saddest in all the world if I knew that I were not in the grace of God."

— Saint Joan of Arc, at her trial, 1431

Her judges asked her a trick question meant to trap her. Her answer showed a faith that was humble, honest, and completely unshaken.
